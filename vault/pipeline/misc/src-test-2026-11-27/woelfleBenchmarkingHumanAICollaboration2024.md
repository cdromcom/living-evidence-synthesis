> [!success] **Summary**
> On PRISMA, a checklist for systematic review reporting, a human and a model matched human consensus on 96% of ratings they both accepted (Table 3, p. 7). Models alone agreed less with consensus than individual human raters across all tools (Table 2, p. 5). Before relying on this approach, test whether it saves reviewer time and check which errors go undetected.


### ❓ Question

- [[sysrev/dgraph/questions/QUE - How accurately can LLMs evaluate research reports' reporting quality, methodological rigor, and trial design|QUE Q-Q1 - How accurately can LLMs evaluate research reports' reporting quality, methodological rigor, and trial design compared with human consensus]] How closely do large language models (LLMs) match human consensus when they assess review reporting, review methods, and clinical trial design?

- [[sysrev/dgraph/questions/QUE - How accurately can LLMs evaluate research reports' methodological rigor using critical appraisal tools|QUE Q-Q2 - How accurately can LLMs evaluate research reports' methodological rigor using critical appraisal tools]] How well can LLMs judge whether systematic reviews used rigorous methods?

- [[sysrev/dgraph/questions/QUE - Does combining several LLM assessments make LLM appraisal of research reports more accurate|QUE Q-Q3 - Does combining several LLM assessments make LLM appraisal of research reports more accurate]] Does combining several model ratings improve agreement with human consensus?

- [[sysrev/dgraph/questions/QUE - Can pairing a human rater with an LLM beat a two-human consensus when appraising research reports|QUE Q-Q4 - Can pairing a human rater with an LLM beat a two-human consensus when appraising research reports]] Can a human and a model reduce the need for a second human appraisal without losing much agreement with human consensus?

Evidence appraisal takes time. A model could help reviewers focus on disputed items, but only if agreement between the model and the first reviewer is a useful signal.


### 🔬 Methods

- **Design.** The authors used existing independent human ratings and their consensus to benchmark model ratings and simulate a workflow that sends disagreements to a second reviewer.

- **Tools.**
  - **PRISMA**, Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses, checks what a review reports.
  - **AMSTAR**, A MeaSurement Tool to Assess systematic Reviews, checks how a review was conducted.
  - **PRECIS-2**, Pragmatic Explanatory Continuum Indicator Summary 2, rates how closely a trial reflects usual care.
  - **Claude-3-Opus**, the only model tested with page images, including figures and tables.
  - **Claude-2**, an older Claude model tested on extracted text.
  - **GPT-4**, an OpenAI model tested on extracted text.
  - **GPT-3.5**, an older OpenAI model tested on extracted text.
  - **Mixtral-8x22B**, an open-source model tested on extracted text.

- **Sample.** The benchmark included 112 pediatric surgery systematic reviews for PRISMA and AMSTAR and 56 randomized PragMeta trials for PRECIS-2 (Section 2.1, p. 2). Analysis counts varied by model and tool, and the second human's PRISMA and AMSTAR ratings were missing for 15 reviews (Table 2, p. 5).

- **Procedure.**
  1. Use the consensus of two independent human raters as the reference (Section 2.1, p. 2).
  2. Give each model the research report and appraisal guidance, and ask it to cite passages and assign ratings.
  3. Measure accuracy as the share of ratings that exactly match human consensus. Report Cohen's kappa, where 0 means chance agreement and 1 means perfect agreement (Section 2.3.1, p. 3).
  4. Pool PRECIS-2 scores 1 and 2, and scores 4 and 5. Use weighted kappa, which gives partial credit for closer scores (Section 2.3.1, p. 3).
  5. Combine nine model assessments and vary how many must agree before keeping a rating (Section 2.3.1, pp. 3–4).
  6. Pair each human rater with each model and keep only ratings where they agree. Count disagreements as deferred, meaning a second human would review them (Section 2.3.1, pp. 4–5).

### 🚧 Caveats

- [[sysrev/dgraph/caveats/CVT - The human consensus datasets are openly online, so the LLMs may have seen them in training, and only a new prospective|CVT V-V1 - The human consensus datasets are openly online, so the LLMs may have seen them in training, and only a new prospective study can rule that out.]] **Possible training overlap.** The consensus data were publicly available. "only prospective replication of these results with new human consensus datasets would eliminate the risk of train/test contamination." (p. 9)

- [[sysrev/dgraph/caveats/CVT - The PRECIS-2 dataset holds mostly pragmatic trials and few explanatory ones, which may explain why GPT-3.5 and|CVT V-V2 - The PRECIS-2 dataset holds mostly pragmatic trials and few explanatory ones, which may explain why GPT-3.5 and Mixtral-8x22B beat larger models.]] **Few explanatory trials.** The mix of trials may have favored models that tend to give pragmatic ratings. "the dataset used in this study to assess PRECIS-2 contains mostly pragmatic trials and few explanatory ones." (p. 9)

- [[sysrev/dgraph/caveats/CVT - The study used older versions of PRISMA and AMSTAR, so results may only partly apply to PRISMA 2020 and AMSTAR-2|CVT V-V3 - The study used older versions of PRISMA and AMSTAR, so results may only partly apply to PRISMA 2020 and AMSTAR-2.]] **Older checklist versions.** The benchmark did not use PRISMA 2020 or AMSTAR-2. These versions may differ enough to change performance. "the current results might only partly apply to them." (p. 9)

- [[sysrev/dgraph/caveats/CVT - Except for Claude-3-Opus, the LLMs read only text and could not see images such as flowcharts|CVT V-V4 - Except for Claude-3-Opus, the LLMs read only text and could not see images such as flowcharts.]] **Unequal access to visuals.** Only Claude-3-Opus saw page images, so the comparison cannot separate model differences from input differences. "the LLMs used could only process text and were blind to images." (p. 9)

- [[sysrev/dgraph/caveats/CVT - Each human consensus rests on only two human experts, so it is an imperfect benchmark and not absolute truth|CVT V-V5 - Each human consensus rests on only two human experts, so it is an imperfect benchmark and not absolute truth.]] **Consensus is not ground truth.** The same raters contributed to the reference used to judge their own individual ratings. "the presented datasets contain ratings from only two human experts" (p. 9)

- [[sysrev/dgraph/caveats/CVT - The datasets did not record how long human raters took, so time savings from human and AI teams cannot be measured|CVT V-V6 - The datasets did not record how long human raters took, so time savings from human and AI teams cannot be measured.]] **No measured time savings.** A second reviewer might still have to read the whole report to rate the disputed items. "This means the efﬁciency and potential time savings cannot be quantiﬁed." (p. 10)

- [[sysrev/dgraph/caveats/CVT - The prompts differed across the LLMs, which makes direct comparisons between individual models harder|CVT V-V7 - The prompts differed across the LLMs, which makes direct comparisons between individual models harder.]] **Prompts differed across models.** The rankings compare models as prompted, not under identical instructions. "While differences in the prompts used complicate comparisons between individual LLMs" (p. 6)
