## Methods Context

### What?

> **Study design:** Component-ablation study within the same supervised benchmark used in the main result, holding the SciBERT backbone and HK+LLMK inputs constant.
>
> **Method type:** Leave-one-component-out ablation on the proposed Ours-SciBERT knowledge-fusion architecture.
>
> **Tools:** Same training and inference stack as the main experiment — SciBERT text encoder, Sparsemax-based Sparse-Attention (Martins & Astudillo 2016), Self-Attention block, Self-Attention Reduction prediction head, Adam optimizer (lr 1e-5, batch size 16, dropout 0.2), V100 / RTX 4090 GPU; HK extracted via Yuan et al. (2022) aspect model; LLMK generated by ChatGPT gpt-3.5-turbo at temperature 0.
>
> **Dependent variables:** Weighted F1 and Accuracy on the test split.
>
> **Independent variable:** Which component is removed — w/o Self-Attention Reduction (SAR), w/o Knowledge-Guided module, or w/o Self-Attention — vs. the full Ours-SciBERT model.
>
> "To better understand the effectiveness of each component of our method, we have done an ablation study as shown in Table 4. For ease of presenting ablation results, we conducted experiments exclusively on the SciBERT-based model, given its superior performance." (Wu et al., 2024, p. 1463)
> ![[wuAutomatedNoveltyEvaluationa-evd-p12-1.png]]

### How?

> **Procedure:** (1) Started from the full Ours-SciBERT model trained with HK+LLMK as inputs (Weighted F1 = 0.83, Accuracy = 0.84). (2) Constructed three ablated variants by removing one component at a time while keeping all other hyperparameters fixed (12 self-attention heads, 6 sparse-attention heads, sparse dimension 128, 5 negative samples, 8:1:1 split): (a) w/o SAR — replaced the Self-Attention Reduction prediction head with a simpler reduction; (b) w/o Knowledge-Guided — removed the Sparse-Attention knowledge-guided module that fuses HK and LLMK features; (c) w/o Self-Attention — removed self-attention applied to LLM (Method) features. (3) Re-trained each variant on the same train split, selected the best checkpoint on the validation split, evaluated on the same held-out test split, and reported Weighted F1 and Accuracy in Table 4. No statistical-significance test reported.
>
> "We systematically removed three components, namely, Self-Attention Reduction, Knowledge Guided, and Self Attention, to examine their individual impacts on the model." (Wu et al., 2024, p. 1463)
> ![[wuAutomatedNoveltyEvaluationa-evd-p12-2.png]]

### Who?

> **Models / participants:** Four model variants — full Ours-SciBERT plus three single-component ablations (w/o SAR, w/o Knowledge-Guided, w/o Self-Attention). All evaluated on the same data as the main experiment; no human raters in this analysis.
>
> **Sample-size flow:** 3,376 ICLR 2022 papers crawled → 2,432 retained after rule-based extraction and exclusion of papers with reviewer TNS disagreement >1 → split 8:1:1 → ≈ 1,945 train / ≈ 243 val / ≈ 244 test. Same test split used for all four ablation variants.
>
> "We split our dataset by a ratio of 8:1:1 for training, validation, and testing." (Wu et al., 2024, p. 1460)
> ![[wuAutomatedNoveltyEvaluationa-evd-p9-3.png]]

## Other Notes

- The Knowledge-Guided module is by far the most load-bearing component: removing it costs 10 F1 points and 10 accuracy points, vs. only 4 / 4 for SAR and 2 / 2 for Self-Attention.
- After removal, the w/o-Knowledge-Guided model (F1 0.73 / Acc 0.74) lands at essentially the same level as the SciBERT PLM baseline using HK+LLMK inputs (F1 0.73 / Acc 0.74) — i.e., the ablation collapses the model to its PLM backbone, supporting the authors' framing that the knowledge-guided module is what carries the fusion gain.
- Ablation reported on a single SciBERT backbone only; not replicated on the other Ours-* variants. No confidence intervals or significance test reported.
